## 4. Stochastic lead time: exact mixture vs variance convolution

Lead time is 2 days 70% of the time and 14 days 30% of the time - a supplier that is usually domestic and occasionally not. Both models have identical mean (560) and standard deviation (554); they disagree about shape.

Share of lead-time demand variance coming from lead-time variability: 98.4%.

| cycle service target | reorder point (exact mixture) | reorder point (moment-matched) | error of moment matching |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.75 | 1,291 | 776 | -40% |
| 0.85 | 1,397 | 1,059 | -24% |
| 0.90 | 1,446 | 1,283 | -11% |
| 0.95 | 1,508 | 1,666 | +10% |
| 0.98 | 1,572 | 2,171 | +38% |
| 0.99 | 1,613 | 2,553 | +58% |

Matching two moments to a unimodal shape cannot represent two modes. It understates the reorder point across the whole service range a planner would actually pick, then overstates it far out in the tail. The understatement is the dangerous one: it is invisible until the long lead time lands.

Validation with a bimodal lead time of 3 days (60%) or 9 days (40%). The column `Q / max lead-time demand` is the condition under which at most one order is outstanding, which is what the single-cycle formula assumes:

| Q | Q / max lead-time demand | target | reorder point | simulated CSL | error |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 800 | 0.89 | 0.90 | 1,018 | 0.8125 | -0.0875 |
| 800 | 0.89 | 0.95 | 1,067 | 0.9044 | -0.0456 |
| 1,500 | 1.67 | 0.90 | 1,018 | 0.9006 | +0.0006 |
| 1,500 | 1.67 | 0.95 | 1,067 | 0.9481 | -0.0019 |

When the lot is smaller than the longest lead-time demand, a second order goes out before the first lands, two orders are exposed to the same demand, and realised service falls well below the formula. Long variable lead times with small lots - which describes most low-volume imported parts - sit squarely in that regime.

## 5. Lot sizing: exact dynamic program vs the heuristics MRP actually runs

50 random 18-period demand series: each period is drawn uniform on [0, 200] and rounded, with setup cost 300 and holding cost 1 per unit per period. Wagner-Whitin is exact, so every gap below is a true optimality gap.

| method | mean gap | median gap | worst gap | mean orders placed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| wagner-whitin | 0.00% | 0.00% | 0.00% | 6.8 |
